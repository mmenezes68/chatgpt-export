## Chapter 3 – What Is a Neural Correlate of Consciousness

### Introduction

Concept of **NCC**: definition, limits, and role in the science of mind.

### Main Ideas

1. **Definition**: present when there is experience and absent when there is none.
    
2. NCCs do not explain the **why** of experience.
    
3. They may vary by modality.
    
4. Challenge: distinguishing correlation from causation.
    
5. Prepare the ground for broader theories.
    

### Key Concepts

- **NCC**; **Presence/Absence Criterion**; **Multiple Realizability**; **Methodological Challenge**.
    

## Reflection Questions

3. Are they universal or organism-dependent?
    
4. Do they approach a complete explanation?

## Chapter 4 – On the Search for the Neural Correlate of Consciousness

### Introduction

Methodologies for searching **NCCs**; criteria, techniques, and limitations.

### Main Ideas

1. **Criterion**: present with experience and absent without experience.
    
2. **Methods**: fMRI, EEG, MEG, TMS, lesions.
    
3. **Challenges**: multiple realizability, individual variation, resolution limits.
    
4. NCCs do not explain the **why**, but they anchor theories.
    
5. Integration with philosophical frameworks.
    

### Key Concepts

- **Presence/Absence Criterion**; **Experimental Tools**; **Methodological Limits**.
    

### Reflection Questions

## Chapter 6 – The Two-Dimensional Argument against Materialism

### Introduction

**Two-dimensional argument** against reductive materialism; distinction between **epistemological** and **metaphysical** possibility.

### Main Ideas

1. Materialism does not show why the physical would imply experience.
    
2. **Philosophical zombies**: physically identical worlds without consciousness are conceivable.
    
3. Epistemic (\(\(\(\(\(\neq\)\)\)\)\) metaphysical.
    
4. If consciousness varies independently, materialism is incomplete.
    
5. Necessity of **psychophysical laws**.
    

### Key Concepts

- **Philosophical Zombies**; **Two-Dimensional Argument**; **Materialist Reductionism**; **Modality/Conceivability**.
    

### Reflection Questions

1. Do zombies refute materialism?
    
2. Does conceivability guide metaphysical possibility?
    
3. Does the argument affect all materialism?
    
4. Role of modal logic?
    
5. Psychophysical laws: solution or hypothesis?
    

### Relevant Passages

> "It is conceivable a world physically identical to ours but without conscious experience." "Even if physicalism is true at the epistemological level, it may fail at the metaphysical level."
